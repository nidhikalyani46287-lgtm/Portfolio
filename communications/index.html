A fintech can hold billions in deposits and still be sitting on one unmanaged AI risk — the model that decides who gets a loan.

Under the EU AI Act, credit scoring isn't a grey area. It's explicitly high-risk. That means documentation, human oversight, and a conformity assessment before the model touches a single application.

And here's the part teams miss: it doesn't replace GDPR — it stacks on top of it. Article 22's rules on automated decisions still apply. You're now answering to both.

The good news? If you already run DPIAs, you're closer than you think. The muscle transfers. I wrote up what the classification actually requires — and a readiness checklist — in the comments. 👇

One term to know

Human oversight

A person who can meaningfully understand, monitor, and override an AI system. It's the antidote to "the computer said no" — but only real if the human has the authority, information, and time to actually intervene. A rubber-stamp click doesn't count.

How I'd measure it

Publishing is half the job; knowing whether it landed is the other half. The metrics I'd track per cycle — and, more importantly, what I'd change based on each one.

Reach
Impressions & follower growth — is the work finding new audiences, or circling the same one?
Engage
Saves, shares, comment quality — shares beat likes. A save means it was useful enough to keep.
Convert
Open & click-through rate, click-to-read — did the newsletter earn the open, and did the post earn the click?

The point of a report isn't the number — it's the next decision. Every metric here maps to something I'd actually do differently next cycle: a reworked hook, a better send time, a topic that clearly resonated.
